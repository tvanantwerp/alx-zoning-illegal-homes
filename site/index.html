    How to read this map

    Each lot with a home was measured against the rules for its zone in the city's Zoning Ordinance (as amended through January 2026): lot size, lot width and street frontage, side and rear yards, floor space, height, open space, and whether that kind of home is allowed in the zone at all.

    These homes are not in trouble. The ordinance lets older buildings that no longer fit the rules stay, and even be rebuilt as they were. "Illegal" here means: if this lot were empty, nobody could get a permit to build what stands on it now.

    Things to keep in mind:

    • Measurements come from the city's public map data, not from surveys. Each check leaves a margin for error, but a finding for any single lot can still be wrong. Yard findings are the least certain, because building outlines are traced from roofs.
    • Front yards are mostly not checked. In most zones the required front yard depends on the neighbors' front yards, so an existing home passes almost by definition.
    • Parking requirements are not checked.
    • Planned districts such as Cameron Station, Potomac Yard and Carlyle were each built under a plan for the whole district, approved by City Council, which replaces the ordinary zone rules there. Homes there are judged by what the ordinance says each plan allows: which kinds of home, how tall, and how many across the district. Lot sizes and yards in these districts are fixed by approved site plan drawings, not by general rules, so they are not judged. Tick "Judge them by ordinary zoning instead" to see how far these neighborhoods are from what the ordinary rules for that land would allow.
    • In Potomac Yard the fallback zone depends on where a lot sits (near Route 1, east of the Metro tracks, or elsewhere). Those boundaries are approximated.
    • Exceptions the city grants to single properties, such as variances and special use permits, are not known to this map. A home shown breaking a rule may have been allowed this way.
