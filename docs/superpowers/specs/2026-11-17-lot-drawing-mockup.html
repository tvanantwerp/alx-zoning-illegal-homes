Six ways to draw a lot's broken rules

Each mockup is the pop-up you'd see after clicking a lot on the map. Idea F, first, combines the parts picked from the others. All six draw real Alexandria lots from the city's data, using the same measurements the map already makes. Pick a lot to see how each idea copes with different problems.

Example lot
IDEA F · COMBINED

3D model with the full report

The spinning 3D model from idea B leads. The height-limit ceiling only appears when the home is too tall. Below it, every rule the map checks is listed with its result (from idea E), next to the lot drawn to scale against the smallest lot the zone allows (from idea C).

Hover a row to pick out that rule's label on the model.

Good for
A strong first picture, with every number one glance below it.
Costs
The tallest of the pop-ups, so it needs to scroll on smaller screens. Still adds the 3D library.
IDEA A

Site plan with numbered callouts

Drawn like a surveyor's plat, with the street along the bottom. Each broken rule gets a numbered red callout on the drawing and a matching line in the list. Hover a line to light up its part of the drawing.

Good for
Seeing every problem at once, with exact numbers.
Costs
Busy on lots with five or more problems. Height only appears in a small side view.
IDEA B

3D model you can spin

The lot lies flat with the street marked along its front, and each building rises to its real height. A see-through ceiling marks the height limit, and anything poking above it turns red. Yards that are too shallow glow red on the ground. Drag to spin, scroll or pinch to zoom.

Good for
Height, and a striking first impression.
Costs
Adds a 3D library (about 600 KB). Exact numbers are harder to read, and dragging can fight with scrolling on phones.
IDEA C

What the rules would allow here

Instead of measuring what's wrong, this draws what you could build today: the green area where a home may stand once the required yards are taken out, and, dashed, the smallest lot the zone allows, laid over the real one. Any part of the home outside the green turns red.

Good for
Making the point fast: the house doesn't fit the rules.
Costs
Floor space doesn't draw on a plan, so it gets side-by-side squares. The buildable area is a new calculation.
IDEA D

One rule at a time

A short walk-through. Each step shows one broken rule: one drawing with only that rule marked, the measured number beside the required one, and a plain sentence. Height steps switch to a side view.

Good for
Phones, and for sharing. Each step could also be saved as an image.
Costs
No single overview. Someone has to click through to see everything.
IDEA E

Full inspection report

Every rule the map checks, passed or failed, with the measured value against the limit. Passing measurements are drawn too, in green. Hover a row to find it on the drawing.

Good for
Homeowners checking whether a finding is right.
Costs
Less dramatic. Passing rules dilute the story.
